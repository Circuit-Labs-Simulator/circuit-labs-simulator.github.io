How to use Circuit Lab

  • Add a part: drag it out of the box on the left (or just click it).
  • Connect: drag an end dot near another end. They snap together and a green ring shows up.
  • Move / reshape: drag the middle to move a part, drag an end to stretch or turn it.
  • Change it: click a part to set its voltage, resistance, thickness or length. Its voltage, current and power show there too.
  • Delete: right-click the part, press Delete, or drag it back to the parts box. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes.
  • Switch: double-click it to open or close it.
  • Battery: click it for the ON / OFF button (or double-click it) to turn it off without deleting it.
  • Short circuit: if a battery is joined end to end with only plain wire (nothing with resistance), the current is huge, so the battery catches fire and burns out after about 3 seconds. Click it to put in a new one. A fuse in the loop saves it.
  • Fuse: it blows (breaks the circuit) when the current goes above its rating. Click it to change the rating or put in a new fuse.
  • Schematic view (top right): shows the same circuit as a diagram with the standard symbols. Press it again to go back.
  • More space: scroll or use − / + (bottom left) to zoom, drag the empty background to slide the board around, and press » to tuck the Circuit Lab box away.
  • Wire Resistance (top left): when it is ON, longer and thinner wires have more resistance, so the current drops and the electrons slow down.
  • Series vs Parallel: press that example to see both side by side. The series bulbs are dimmer (they share the voltage). The parallel bulbs are brighter, but the battery gives twice the current, so its charge bar empties faster.
  • Meters (top right): turn one on, then drag it and its probes. Ammeter: hold the ring over any part to read its current. Voltmeter: touch the red (–) and black (+) probes to two spots to read the voltage between them. Ohmmeter: touch its two probes to two spots to read the resistance between them (it ignores the batteries, like measuring with the power off).
  • Electrons vs conventional: electrons (blue –) really flow from – to +. Conventional current (red arrows) is drawn from + to –.
